## The short version

**Stance: conditional.** This is a cheap way into one of the better locations in Broward County: a low-rise on the barrier island between the Intracoastal and the ocean, a block from the sand and a five-minute walk to the Pompano Beach pier and Fishing Village. The price has been cut 25% in eight months, the market favors buyers, and the asking price per square foot is below the city's condo average.

Three things decide whether to buy, in order:

1. **Can it be financed with a normal mortgage?** Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac ended the fast "Limited Review" for condos on August 3, 2026. A 63-year-old coastal building with a $175 fee is the profile that fails the full review (reserves, insurance, deferred maintenance). If it fails, you pay cash or take a portfolio loan at a higher rate, and so does whoever buys from you later. Have your lender run the project review before the financing contingency expires.
2. **What is the association hiding behind a $175 fee?** That is about 19 cents per square foot per month. A building this age on the coast, paying for a master wind policy, water, and a roof fund, usually needs 50 to 60 cents. Either reserves are waived (legal for buildings under three stories), insurance is thin, or the fee is about to jump. Get the budget, the balance sheet, the master policy declarations page, and 24 months of minutes.
3. **What did the engineers find?** Broward County requires a structural and electrical recertification at 40 years and every 10 years after. This building was due in 2003, 2013 and 2023. The 2023 report is the single most useful document you can read, and the 2033 cycle is the next predictable bill.

What will cost money: a drain-line repipe (cast iron from 1963 is past its life), a roof at some point in the next decade, insurance that rises faster than inflation, and property taxes that reset to your purchase price. Model about $2,600/month all-in at 20% down today and $3,100/month by 2036, plus $20,000 to $40,000 of special assessments across that decade if the building is not already funding them.

The long-term bet is the land. An 0.82-acre barrier-island parcel under a two-story 1963 building, a block from the beach and a few blocks from a planned W hotel-condo tower, is worth more to a developer than the sum of its units. That cuts both ways: a windfall if the owners vote to terminate and sell, a forced exit if you are in the minority.

An association is registered with the city at 3204 Colony Club Rd Apt 3.
- **Construction.** Concrete block is typical for the era; verify. A neighboring unit's public record shows a 1988 renovation year, the probable condo-conversion or refurbishment era for the complex.
- **Age-driven systems, all unknown status:**
  - *Drain lines.* Cast iron was standard before 1975 and is rated for about 50 years; on slab-on-grade coastal buildings it often fails sooner. At 63 years, assume original unless shown otherwise. Building-wide repipes in small South Florida buildings run $8,000 to $25,000 per unit. Pay for a sewer camera scope of the unit line.
  - *Electrical.* Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels of this era are uninsurable with most Florida carriers. Photograph the unit panel and the meter room; get the electrical recertification date.
  - *Roof.* Flat modified-bitumen roofs last about 18 years and carriers now surcharge or refuse roofs past 15 to 20 years. Pull the roof permit history from the city permit portal.
  - *Windows and doors.* Impact rating drives your HO-6 wind premium and the master policy. Ask for the wind mitigation report (form OIR-B1-1802).
  - *Water heater, HVAC.* Ask ages; budget replacement at 11 and 14 years respectively.
- **Parking.** One carport space per unit, per a rental listing in the same building.

## 3. The association and Florida condo law

Florida's post-Surfside laws (SB 4-D 2022, SB 154 2023, HB 913 2025) require a structural **milestone inspection** and a **Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS)**, with reserves that cannot be waived, for buildings **three or more habitable stories**. A two-story building is exempt from both. That is not good news in disguise: exempt buildings may still **waive reserves by majority vote** (Fla. Stat. 718.112(2)(f)), which is the most common way a 1963 building keeps a $175 fee.

Broward County's own **40/50-year recertification** program (structural and electrical, every ten years after year 40) applies regardless of height. Cycles for this building: 2003, 2013, 2023, **next 2033**.

**What you are entitled to before closing (Fla. Stat. 718.503(2)), at the seller's expense:** declaration, articles, bylaws, rules; the most recent year-end financial statement and current budget; the milestone inspection summary or a statement that none is required; the SIRS or a statement that none is required; the FAQ sheet; and 12 months of approved board minutes. You then have **3 business days** to cancel after receiving them. The estoppel certificate (what the unit owes) is capped at $250 ($100 more for a 3-day rush) and must be delivered within 10 business days.

**Unknown and essential:**

## 4. Financing and resale liquidity

- **Rates.** 30-year fixed averaged 7.32% on October 16, 2026 (Zillow); Freddie Mac's weekly was 7.28% on October 1.
- **Warrantability.** Lender Letter LL-2026-03 (Fannie) and the matching Freddie bulletin retired the Limited Review on August 3, 2026. Full review requires reserves of at least 10% of the annual budget (15% for loans dated on or after January 4, 2027), adequate master insurance, no significant deferred maintenance, and delinquency under 15%. A project that fails is non-warrantable: cash or portfolio lending only.
- **Why this matters beyond your loan.** The pool of buyers who can pay cash for a $300,000 beach condo is smaller than the pool who can finance one. A non-warrantable verdict lowers your resale price and lengthens your market time for as long as the building stays non-warrantable.
- **Appraisal risk.** No Zestimate, thin sales in the building, and conflicting square footage all raise the odds of an appraisal below contract.

At $2,100/month: gross yield 8.4%; after 8% vacancy, 8% management, HOA, non-homestead taxes and insurance, the cap rate is about 3.5% and net operating income about $10,500/yr. With a 20%-down mortgage at 7.32%, monthly cash flow is about **negative $780**. This is a lifestyle or land purchase, not an income purchase, unless bought with cash or at a materially lower price.

Short-term rental: Pompano Beach requires an annual permit ($675 initial, $375 renewal), a 24-hour contact within 25 miles, city inspections, and two guests per bedroom. The association's minimum term governs regardless; most small buildings here set 30 to 90 days.

## 7. Location

- **Where it is.** Pompano Beach's "Beach" neighborhood, on the barrier island between the Intracoastal Waterway and the Atlantic. About a block to the sand; about 1,500 ft to the Pompano Beach pier and the Fishing Village (Beach House, Oceanic, Lucky Fish, the Briny Irish Pub). Hillsboro Inlet, the 1907 lighthouse, Hillsboro Inlet Park and the charter fleet ("Sailfish Alley") are about a mile north. Lauderdale-by-the-Sea is a few minutes south.
- **Walkability.** Walk Score 42 to 56 on this street (car-dependent to somewhat walkable), Transit 27 to 30, Bike 52. Groceries (Publix) and Pompano Citi Centre are across the Intracoastal on Federal Highway.
- **Getting around.** I-95 via Atlantic Blvd in about 12 minutes; downtown Fort Lauderdale about 30 minutes; FLL about 35; Boca Raton about 25. No rail on the island: Tri-Rail's Pompano station is at NW 8th Ave and 35th St; Brightline stops in Boca Raton and Fort Lauderdale. Broward County Transit route 11 runs A1A.
- **Schools.** Broward County Public Schools; assignment for this address must be confirmed with the district's locator. Pompano Beach High (A-rated) is an all-magnet school by application, not by address. Cresthaven Elementary (33064) earned a B in 2024-25 after several C years.
- **Noise.** Pompano Beach Airpark is about 2.5 miles west with a history of helicopter-training noise complaints, mostly over neighborhoods near the field. A1A traffic and the Fishing Village are the local sources.
- **Who lives here (ZIP 33062, ACS 2024).** Population about 24,900; median age about 60; median household income about $83,600; 72% owner-occupied; about 37% of units vacant, which is seasonal and second-home stock rather than distress. Expect a quiet summer and a full winter.
- **Crime.** Citywide property crime about 21 per 1,000 (2024 FBI data) and sources disagree on whether the violent rate is above or below national. Incidents concentrate inland and in the northwest; the beach is among the quieter areas, with car break-ins at beach parking the common complaint. Pompano Beach is policed by the Broward Sheriff's Office.

## 8. Hazards, now and later

- **Evacuation Zone A** (east of the Intracoastal): mandatory evacuation for every Category 1 or stronger hurricane threatening the county. Plan for shutters, a place to go, and days without power on return.
- **FEMA flood zone: *unconfirmed*.** New coastal maps took effect July 31, 2024. Barrier-island parcels here are usually AE (base flood elevation about 6 to 7 ft NAVD) with X pockets on the A1A ridge. Zone sets whether a lender requires flood insurance and what NFIP charges. Look it up at the FEMA Map Service Center, then confirm with the city Building Division (954-786-4669, floodzoneinfo@copbfl.com) and get the elevation certificate. Which floor the unit is on changes the risk and the premium.
- **Wind.** First Street rates wind risk here extreme with a 99% chance of a damaging wind event in 30 years. Wilma (2005) stripped roofs across Pompano and caused a boil-water order; Irma (2017) hit the whole east coast; Nicole (2022) washed out trucked-in sand from NE 16th St north to the inlet.
- **Sea level.** The Unified Southeast Florida projection: +10 to 17 inches by 2040, +21 to 54 inches by 2070 (from 2000 levels). The local tide gauge trend is about 4 mm/yr. On this island it shows up first as king-tide street flooding near the Intracoastal and salt in the stormwater system, then as insurance and resale friction. Broward's seawall standard rises to 5 ft NAVD by 2050. The city's March 2025 Vulnerability Assessment maps sunny-day flooding by street; check it for Colony Club Rd.
- **Heat.** Rated extreme; cooling is most of a Florida electric bill and a 1963 envelope is a poor one.
- **Beach.** The stretch nearest Hillsboro Inlet erodes fastest; Broward and the Army Corps re-nourish on a multi-year cycle.

## 13. Who this suits

- **Primary residence** for someone who wants to walk to the beach and the pier, can absorb $2,600 to $3,200 a month plus occasional five-figure assessments, and will file for homestead. Best fit.
- **Snowbird / second home**: workable; taxes run about 20% higher without homestead, and the 37% seasonal vacancy in the ZIP means a quiet summer.
- **Rental investor**: negative cash flow at today's rates; only works with cash or a materially lower price, and only after confirming the rental minimum.
- **Anyone who needs conventional financing and a quick resale**: wait for the lender's project review before falling in love.
