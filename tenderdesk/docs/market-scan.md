# Canada Market Scan, 2026–2031: Where the Gap Is

*Research compiled August 20, 2026 from three parallel deep-dives (macro outlook, niche
validation, procurement deep-dive). All figures CAD unless noted; sources inline.*

## 1. The economy you're starting into

Canada entered 2026 in a technical recession (Q4 2025 −1.0% annualized, Q1 2026 −0.1%) and is
now in a shallow recovery: the Bank of Canada projects **0.7% growth in 2026, ~1.8% in
2027–28** ([BoC July 2026 MPR](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/publications/mpr/mpr-2026-07-15/)),
with the structural 2029–2031 baseline around 1.6–1.8%
([PBO](https://www.pbo-dpb.ca/en/publications/RP-2627-002-S--economic-fiscal-outlook-june-2026--perspectives-economiques-financieres-juin-2026)).
The policy rate is holding at 2.25% with gradual hikes expected from ~2027. Unemployment has
eased to 6.4% (July 2026). The US tariff war is unresolved: the Supreme Court struck the IEEPA
tariffs in February 2026, but Section 232 tariffs (steel 50%, autos 25%) remain, Washington
declined to renew USMCA as-is (July 1, 2026), and a framework deal was still being finalized
the week of August 17–21, 2026. **Plan for continued trade volatility.**

Three structural facts define the next five years:

1. **Private demand is weak.** CFIB members' #1 growth barrier is now *insufficient domestic
   demand* (54%), and 40% of small exporters were exposed to the new 50% tariff threats
   ([CFIB](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/media/new-50-percent-us-tariff-on-cusma-compliant-goods-to-hit-canadian-small-exporters-hard)).
2. **Public capital is the growth engine.** Budget 2025 committed **$280B over five years** in
   capital investment. Defense is ramping from 2% of GDP (~$63B, hit in FY2025-26) toward the
   NATO 5%-by-2035 pledge — the Defence Industrial Strategy frames **$180B in defence
   procurement opportunities over 10 years** with an explicit SME pull-through ($4B BDC
   defence platform, $357.7M Regional Defence Investment Initiative)
   ([PMO](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/02/17/prime-minister-carney-launches-canadas-first-defence-industrial)).
   Housing adds Build Canada Homes ($13B, first six direct-build sites breaking ground
   2026), plus $51B Build Communities Strong, $6B trade-corridor/Arctic infrastructure.
3. **Labour and productivity are the constraints.** Construction loses ~270,000 workers to
   retirement by 2034 ([BuildForce](https://www.on-sitemag.com/features/2026-forecast-canadas-construction-crossroads/));
   76% of small-business owners plan to exit within a decade with $2T+ of assets in play and
   only 9% holding a succession plan
   ([CFIB](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/media/over-2-trillion-in-business-assets-are-at-stake-as-majority-of-small-business-owners-plan-to-exit-their-business-over-the-next-decade));
   86% of finance hiring managers can't fill accounting roles. Productivity growth has averaged
   0.5%/yr since 2019 — which is why every level of government is now funding AI adoption
   (the ~$2B "AI for All" strategy, BDC's LIFT program).

**AI adoption context:** 19.2% of Canadian businesses used AI in Q2 2026, tripling in two years
— but **construction sits at 9.2%, wholesale 7.9%, agriculture 4.5%**
([StatsCan](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/36-28-0001/2026004/article/00002-eng.htm)).
The under-penetrated sectors are exactly where the public money is about to flow.

## 2. The gap

**Canadian public procurement is ~$200B/year (~10% of GDP)** across federal (~$37–51B),
provincial ($100B+), and municipal/MASH ($75B+) buyers. Yet the SME share of federal contract
value has **fallen from 38% in 2008 to ~20% in 2024**, and only ~5% of SME sales touch
government at all. The Procurement Ombud's own reports name the cause: responses take 30–60
hours, RFPs run 100+ pages, and small firms can't afford bid teams
([OPO 2024-25, "Time for Solutions"](https://opo-boa.gc.ca/rapports-reports/2024-2025/index-eng.html)).

Then 2026 happened. Three brand-new policies are actively pushing tender flow toward small
Canadian suppliers:

- **Buy Canadian Policy** (Dec 16, 2025; expanded to contracts ≥$5M on June 15, 2026) gives
  Canadian suppliers evaluation preference in strategic federal procurement
  ([CanadaBuys](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/en/buy-canadian-policy)).
- **Small Business Procurement Program** (July 2026): every federal procurement between
  **$10K and $5M must now be designed "with small businesses in mind"**
  ([PSPC](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-services-procurement/news/2026/07/public-services-and-procurement-canada-launches-new-small-business-procurement-program-measures-to-modernize-federal-contracting-and-boost-access-f.html)).
- **Provincial mirrors**: Ontario's Bill 72 (Ontario-first procurement), BC's near-ban on US
  suppliers, plus the 5% Indigenous procurement minimum ($1.24B+ awarded in 2023-24).

Demand for small Canadian bidders is being *manufactured by policy* at the exact moment most
small firms still can't afford to bid. That mismatch is the gap.

## 4. The winner: a done-for-you "win government work" desk for small Canadian suppliers

**What it is:** an AI-first service (not another discovery feed) that finds the right tenders
for a small contractor/supplier, gives a blunt bid/no-bid verdict, and produces the full
compliance-first bid response — plus the prequalification paperwork (COR, ISNetworld/Avetta,
security-clearance tracking) that gates bidding in the first place.

**Why the crowd missed it:** the dozen Canadian startups that appeared since 2025 (Publicus
$250/mo, MapleTenders, Awardly, GovBid, BidWatch, BidFit...) all built *discovery feeds* — a
race to the bottom against free CanadaBuys email alerts. The money is in the **30–60 hours of
writing and compliance assembly per bid**. That layer is fulfillment work, unattractive to
VCs, and it is precisely what Claude now does at ~99% quality with a human closing the loop.

**Willingness to pay is proven:** human bid consultants charge **$2,450–2,900 per single
bid** (The Bid Lab US$245/hr with a 10-hour minimum; Canadian shops ~$1,500 intake +
$2,900/proposal); enterprise software starts at $20–30K/yr. A small supplier chasing a $500K
contract rationally pays $1,000–2,500 per bid or a few hundred dollars monthly. Nobody serves
them at that price with actual drafting.

**MVP data feasibility: confirmed.** CanadaBuys publishes every open federal tender as a free
daily CSV (with full RFP attachment URLs) under the Open Government Licence — no API key, no
scraping ([open data](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/en/support/opendata)). Québec's SEAO
publishes OCDS JSON; Alberta and Toronto have free JSON APIs. The engine in this repo already
ingests, matches, qualifies, and drafts against this data.

**Risks, honestly:** discovery-layer competitors could move down into drafting (Publicus and
Awardly gesture at it) — the defense is service quality, verticalization (trades first), and
the prequal bundle; AI hallucination in a bid is a disqualifier — hence the engine's hard
no-fabrication/`[FILL:]` design and a human review pass on every deliverable; and government
sales cycles mean clients may wait months for a win — price on deliverables, not outcomes.
